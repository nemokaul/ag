Chapter 1 Introduction to Generative AI and RAG

1.1 Overview of Generative AI

1.1.1 Definition and Significance

Generative artificial intelligence (AI) is a type of AI that learns from existing data and uses that knowledge to make new things, like text, images, music, or code. Generative models make new data points that look like the training data, while discriminative models only focus on classifying or predicting outcomes. This ability is game-changing because it lets machines copy how people are creative and solve problems in ways that were thought to be impossible before. Generative AI is a key part of modern AI applications and is pushing new ideas in fields like healthcare, entertainment, education, and more.

Figure 1.1 shows the difference between discriminative and generative models. On the one side, it shows a discriminative model classifying data (e.g., a cat vs. a dog), and on the other side, it shows a generative model creating new data (e.g., generating an image of a cat).

Generative AI is essential because it can make creative processes quicker and better, which saves time and money and pushes the limits of what machines can do. For example, it can transform text descriptions into realistic pictures, write articles, make music, or even code software. This technology is changing how content is made and making it possible to have personalized experiences like custom learning materials or marketing campaigns. Generative AI is also a big part of the progress that is being made in other areas of AI, like computer vision and natural language processing (NLP). This is why it is an important tool for solving hard problems in the real world.

But generative AI’s main advantage goes beyond automation; it assists people come up with new ideas by giving them new ways to think about problems, try out different scenarios, and find solutions. Generative AI is changing how we use technology and offering us new ways to be creative and grow. It helps scientists work faster and artists express themselves more easily. It will become more important as it becomes more common in everyday apps. This will change industries and make people’s lives better.

1.1.2 Evolution from Rule-Based Systems to Deep Learning

AI started with rule-based systems, which were programmed to follow clear rules and logic to do certain tasks. Early chatbots like ELIZA and other systems like them depended on pre-written instructions and couldn’t learn from data or deal with complicated, unstructured inputs. They worked well for small tasks, but they were inflexible and had trouble with uncertainty or change in real life.
